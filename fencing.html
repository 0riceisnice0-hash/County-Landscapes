A GUIDE TO YOUR ENQUIRY

Before
we begin.

Useful things to think about, whether you have a clear plan or are just exploring the options.

Talk it through ↗
01

Think beyond the panel

Privacy, appearance and the relationship with the rest of the garden are all worth discussing. Bring a reference picture if you have a particular style in mind, and explain what is not working about the current fence.

A boundary beside a seating area might have different priorities from one alongside a side passage. Mention any changes in ground level or awkward corners along the run.

02

Repair a section or rethink the run?

A damaged panel may be the obvious issue, but the condition of the surrounding fence matters too. Describe any movement in the posts and whether neighbouring sections have also deteriorated.

Photographs along the run, rather than only of the damaged point, help provide context. Discuss which existing elements could remain and which would need to be included in the proposed work.

03

Include the practical details

Approximate length is a useful starting point. So are access from each side, planting close to the fence and any old fencing that would need to be taken away.

Make sure the intended boundary line is clear before arranging work. If a gate is part of your plans, raise it in the enquiry so its opening, position and relationship to the fencing can be discussed.

FENCING / QUESTIONS

A few
useful answers.

Can I ask about a small repair?

Yes. Describe the damaged section and the condition of the adjacent fence. Photos of the posts and the full run will help the initial conversation.

What if the garden slopes?

Mention changes in level and include a photograph along the boundary. The way the fence follows the ground needs to be considered before the scope is agreed.

Should I arrange access with my neighbour?

Tell us what access is available on both sides. If neighbouring access would be needed, discuss this before booking the work rather than assuming it will be available.
