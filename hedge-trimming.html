A GUIDE TO YOUR ENQUIRY

Before
we begin.

Useful things to think about, whether you have a clear plan or are just exploring the options.

Talk it through ↗
01

Maintenance or a bigger change?

Keeping an existing outline tidy is different from asking for a significant change in height or width. Explain which you have in mind and what you want the hedge to achieve in the garden.

You might want to open up a path while retaining privacy, or tidy the top without changing the overall height. Describing that outcome is more useful than simply asking for the hedge to be cut back.

02

Show the full length

Include the approximate length and height, and say whether both sides need attention. A photo taken along the hedge helps show its depth and any changes in level.

Mention sheds, beds, ponds, fences or other obstacles at its base. The space available to work and the route for removing clippings are useful details for the quote discussion.

03

Agree the finish and timing

Talk through the intended shape before the work starts, including how the top and ends should look. If you know what type of hedge you have, include that in the enquiry.

The condition of the hedge and what is happening around it can affect the appropriate timing and approach. Ask about the collection of clippings and how the surrounding area will be left.

Useful details to have ready

  1. Approximate height, width and length
  2. One side or both sides to be trimmed
  3. A tidy-up or a more substantial size change
  4. Obstacles and access alongside the hedge
  5. Your preferred timing and handling of clippings

No need to have every answer. Start with what you know.

HEDGE TRIMMING / QUESTIONS

A few
useful answers.

Can I keep the height but reduce the width?

Describe the shape you want and the reason for the change. The appropriate work depends on the hedge and its condition, which can be discussed before anything is agreed.

Do both sides have to be included?

Tell us which faces of the hedge need attention and what access is available. The agreed scope should make clear which sides and the top are included.

Should I send measurements?

Rough dimensions are useful; they do not need to be a detailed survey. Include photos and mention any sections that are taller or harder to reach.
