A GUIDE TO YOUR ENQUIRY

Before
we begin.

Useful things to think about, whether you have a clear plan or are just exploring the options.

Talk it through ↗
01

Start with the way you use your garden

Perhaps the seating area catches the wrong end of the day, the lawn is awkward to look after, or different parts of the garden feel disconnected. Describe the problem first. You do not need to arrive with a finished design.

Make a short list of what you want to keep, what needs to go, and what matters most. A useful outside space might combine somewhere to sit, a clear walking route, room for planting and space for everyday storage.

02

Look at the ground before the finish

The visible finish is only part of a landscaping project. Existing levels, how water moves through the garden and the condition of the ground all need to be understood before a scope can be agreed.

Bring up narrow access, steps, slopes and any areas that stay wet. If materials or equipment would need to pass through the house, mention that at the start. These details help make the discussion more realistic.

03

Agree where the project begins and ends

A garden refresh can mean very different things. Talk through which areas are included, the finishes you have in mind, what happens to existing materials and which details you would like priced separately.

If you are working to a budget, share a comfortable range and your priorities. Discuss whether the work could be split into stages, and make sure each stage would leave you with a usable space.

LANDSCAPING / QUESTIONS

A few
useful answers.

Do I need a garden design before I call?

No. Start with how you want to use the garden and the problems you want to solve. Reference pictures and rough measurements are useful, but a finished plan is not needed for an initial conversation.

Can I keep parts of the existing garden?

Tell us which plants, boundaries, surfaces or features you would like to retain. Working around existing elements should be part of the discussion from the beginning.

Can landscaping and fencing be discussed together?

Yes. If changes to the boundary are part of your plans, include them in the same enquiry so the order and scope of the work can be considered together.
