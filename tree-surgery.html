A GUIDE TO YOUR ENQUIRY

Before
we begin.

Useful things to think about, whether you have a clear plan or are just exploring the options.

Talk it through ↗
01

Describe the concern, not just the cut

Is a tree taking up more space than you expected? Are you worried about a particular branch, or considering removal as part of a garden change? Explain what has prompted your enquiry.

A photograph of the whole tree and another showing the area around its base can give useful context. Only take photographs from somewhere you can safely stand; there is no need to climb or approach a tree you are concerned about.

02

The surroundings matter

Buildings, fences, neighbouring gardens and the route into the site are part of the job, not just background detail. Mention nearby structures and any restricted access when you get in touch.

The amount of space around a tree affects how the work can be approached. An initial description helps establish what needs to be assessed before the work and its cost can be agreed.

03

Be clear about the complete job

Tree surgery, felling and what is left afterwards are different parts of a conversation. Ask exactly what is included in the proposed work and what condition the area will be left in.

Discuss branches, logs, waste removal and any remaining stump separately. Do not assume a felling enquiry includes stump removal. Mention any known restrictions or permissions affecting the tree so these can be considered before work is arranged.

Useful details to have ready

  1. Your reason for getting in touch
  2. The approximate size of the tree, if known
  3. Photographs taken from a safe position
  4. Nearby buildings, fences and access restrictions
  5. Whether the enquiry includes removal of cut material

No need to have every answer. Start with what you know.

TREE CARE / QUESTIONS

A few
useful answers.

Can you quote from a photograph?

A photograph is a useful starting point, but it does not show every aspect of a tree or its surroundings. A visit may be needed before the scope and price can be confirmed.

Should I identify the tree first?

If you know the species, include it. If you do not, say so and describe the tree or provide photographs from a safe position.

Does felling include the stump?

Ask about this explicitly. The treatment of the remaining stump should be agreed as part of the scope rather than assumed.
